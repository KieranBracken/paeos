## Executive disposition

The archive contains a coherent research trajectory rather than an implementation: it begins
with mathematics as a navigable concept space, expands reasoning into a portfolio of search
processes, makes discovery distinct from retrieval, and finally compares that graph-centred
architecture with a compression-centred alternative. Its durable contribution is not any single
speculative mechanism. It is the conjunction of four ideas:

1. concepts require more than one representation;
2. reasoning benefits from diverse, cooperating search strategies;
3. analogy and recombination are useful candidate generators; and
4. proposal, verification, and authoritative knowledge must remain distinct.

CEO-1 preserves those contributions while resolving the archive's ambiguities against the
canonical Ecosystem Constitution, including the mission-cited §4.3/§4.4 boundary. Concept OS is
the sovereign owner of conceptual topology,
transformations, analogies, semantic neighborhoods, exploration traces, and candidate
hypotheses. The Concept Graph is its principal durable substrate. The Inspiration Engine is an
internal subsystem. Concept OS may measure relevance, novelty, and serendipity, but those values
are heuristics, not truth confidence. PAEOS owns governed execution and evidence collection;
SAKG alone validates or falsifies engineering claims; Sentium alone owns trading action.

The Phase 3 recommendation to demote the graph to a disposable index is therefore not adopted as
an authority-level decision. The Constitution explicitly makes the Concept Graph the principal
durable substrate. Its useful MDL, wake/sleep, graded-warrant, and learned-scheduling ideas remain
recorded as later hypotheses behind CEO-1 interfaces rather than being silently discarded.

## 2. Archaeological strata

### 2.1 Stratum A — concept-space origin

The earliest architecture is relational and navigational. Mathematics is treated as a landscape;
the sliding-puzzle analogy adds prerequisites, ordering, and local moves; reasoning becomes a
path through concepts, transformations, abstractions, and hypotheses. The important residue is
the notion that conceptual progress has topology and path provenance. The weak assumption is
that the relevant concept space already exists and is sufficiently connected.

**CEO-1 disposition:** preserve explicit topology, typed prerequisites, paths, and constraints.
Do not claim that the initial graph is complete or naturally given.

### 2.2 Stratum B — active and hybrid concepts

The archive repeatedly moves from labels to passive nodes, rich objects, executable objects,
living entities, transformations, agents, compression units, probability distributions, and a
hybrid representation. This is less a sequence of replacements than a discovery that several
different concerns had been collapsed into the word *concept*:

- identity and semantics;
- representations used by different algorithms;
- operations that transform one state into another;
- runtime actors and scheduling;
- learned scores and uncertainty; and
- revision history.

**CEO-1 disposition:** separate these concerns. A `Concept` is an immutable semantic record.
Representations are typed facets or content-addressed references. Behavior is reified as a
`Transformation` operator. Scheduling belongs to the Search Orchestra. History is append-only
provenance and supersession. Concepts do not become autonomous agents in CEO-1.

### 2.3 Stratum C — Search Orchestra

The archive rejects a single universal search algorithm. It proposes problem compilation,
constraint reduction, diverse candidate generation, a shared candidate pool, Monte Carlo
expansion, evolutionary mutation, neural ranking, symbolic verification, and graph update. Its
algorithm inventory is:

- A* and other cost-aware graph search;
- beam search;
- deterministic or seeded random walks;
- bidirectional search;
- Monte Carlo tree search;
- evolutionary mutation and crossover;
- constraint propagation and graph rewriting;
- embedding or neural ranking; and
- symbolic verification.

**CEO-1 disposition:** preserve a pluggable, plural search portfolio and shared deduplicated
frontier. Start with bounded graph traversal, A*/best-first, beam, bidirectional, and seeded
random exploration. Treat MCTS, evolutionary strategies, neural ranking, and learned allocation
as later plugins. Replace unbounded or wall-clock-sensitive behavior with deterministic
expansion, node, hop, and result budgets. Remove engineering-truth verification from Concept OS;
only structural well-formedness and query constraints are checked locally.

### 2.4 Stratum D — Discovery and Inspiration

The discovery material contains five concrete generator families:

1. **gap detection:** propose a missing relation where two subgraphs are structurally parallel;
2. **cross-domain analogy:** map roles and relations across domains;
3. **recombination:** compose concepts or successful partial paths;
4. **abstraction formation:** generalize, specialize, compress, or name recurring structure; and
5. **hypothesis generation:** state a falsifiable candidate and preserve its rationale.

It also identifies structural similarity, topology, embedding distance, reasoning history,
successful paths, recurring transformations, compression, mutation, and random exploration as
signals. The archive correctly says that failure is informative, but its early pipeline risks
turning a generated edge into knowledge after an internal verification step.

**CEO-1 disposition:** preserve every generator as a heuristic proposer. Reify mappings and
transformation traces, retain rejected candidates, and label every score exploratory. Candidate
hypotheses leave Concept OS through a typed bridge; they do not become SAKG truth within the
discovery loop.

### 2.5 Stratum E — Compression Core synthesis

The comparative review supplies a competing organizing principle: minimize description length
over a library of executable hypotheses; use plural proposers; admit only verified compression
improvements; and consolidate during a wake/sleep cycle. It also offers useful classifications:
fixed core, emergent fluid knowledge, and reconstructible condensate.

Its strongest contributions are an explicit cost signal, the separation of proposal from
admission, and the recognition that local edge reweighting is not the same as abstraction. Its
weakest move, under the now-canonical boundary map, is treating the graph as a disposable Tier-2
index and the executable hypothesis library as the real substrate. It also assumes that one MDL
objective can stand in for scientific usefulness and that verification can occur inside the same
system that proposes.

**CEO-1 disposition:** retain compression gain as an optional versioned `InspirationHeuristic`,
not a constitutional objective or truth gate. Defer wake/sleep consolidation and executable
hypothesis interpretation until experiments show value. Preserve the Concept Graph as durable
canonical Concept OS state.

## 4. Ontology reconciliation

### 4.1 Concept

A concept is a stable, immutable unit of semantic identity within a namespace. It carries a
minimal symbolic payload and zero or more typed representations. It is not a claim that the
definition is true, not an agent, and not executable code. A changed semantic payload produces a
new deterministic URI and an explicit `supersedes` derivation.

### 4.2 Representation

A representation is an immutable facet consumed by an algorithm, not another sovereign object.
CEO-1 permits symbolic/lexical data, structural graph context, exemplar references, vector
references, and procedural-affordance references. Vector values and model outputs are
accelerators and must record model/version/content hashes. Missing representation kinds degrade
an algorithm explicitly; they do not silently become zero similarity.

### 4.3 Operator and transformation

An operator is the typed, versioned rule within a `Transformation` node. Its signature,
preconditions, postconditions, parameters, determinism class, and implementation reference are
data. The store never executes arbitrary code from a node. Pure local operators may be applied by
an allow-listed Concept OS executor; model-backed or externally acting operations must be
requested through PAEOS.

### 4.4 Analogy

An analogy is a first-class mapping object, not a transitive similarity edge. It identifies source
and target roles, explicit correspondences, preserved relations, known breaks, distance snapshots,
and the heuristic that proposed it. This permits n-ary analogies and prevents `A analogous B` plus
`B analogous C` from silently asserting `A analogous C`.

### 4.5 Hypothesis

A hypothesis is a falsifiable candidate proposition with declared scope, assumptions,
transformation/analogy lineage, expected observations, and falsifiers. Its Concept OS lifecycle
may be `draft`, `candidate`, `exported`, `dismissed`, or `superseded`; `validated`, `proven`, and
`canonical truth` are forbidden Concept OS statuses. SAKG may later record a separate disposition
against the exported candidate.

## 5. Constitutional boundary alignment

| Concern | Concept OS owns | Boundary that must not be crossed |
|---|---|---|
| Conceptual state | Concepts, representations, exploratory relations, analogies, transformations, neighborhoods, search traces, heuristics, and hypotheses. | It cannot mutate PAEOS tasks/ledgers, SAKG claims, or Sentium orders. |
| Discovery | Candidate generation, relevance/novelty/serendipity scoring, and possibility-space exploration. | Scores cannot promote a claim, authorize an experiment, or trigger a trade. |
| Execution | Pure, local, allow-listed graph and operator computation. | Provider selection, retries, sandboxes, external tools, and side effects belong to PAEOS. |
| Knowledge input | Read-only SAKG validated mechanisms, constraints, contradictions, and open questions by typed URI/content hash. | A copied SAKG reference does not transfer SAKG authority into Concept OS. |
| Candidate output | Immutable candidate-hypothesis envelopes and exploration provenance. | Concept OS cannot call a SAKG promotion endpoint or label an export validated. |
| Trading | May form abstract hypotheses about market concepts. | It cannot create orders, risk decisions, strategies, or execution instructions for Sentium. |

The compliant closed loop is:

```text
SAKG validated knowledge (read-only)
        -> Concept OS exploration and candidate hypothesis
        -> PAEOS-governed experiment / evidence collection
        -> SAKG validate, narrow, falsify, or promote
        -> Concept OS reads the new validated boundary
```

The SAKG emission bridge in CEO-1 is therefore **candidate-only and mediated**. Concept OS emits a
content-addressed envelope to an append-only outbox. PAEOS admits any experiment and binds
evidence. A SAKG adapter may ingest the envelope as a candidate record, but only SAKG may decide
claim status. There is no direct Concept OS-to-SAKG database write.

## 6. Forensic findings and contradictions

### F-01 — The archive is architecture research, not implementation evidence

The documents contain no production package, database, schema, benchmark harness, or empirical
discovery result. CEO-1 must start with falsifiable slices and must not inherit “current
architecture” language as proof.

### F-02 — “Verification is essential” survives, but verification authority moves

The archive places symbolic verification inside Concept OS. The Constitution assigns engineering
truth to SAKG and governed execution/evidence to PAEOS. CEO-1 retains local schema, constraint,
and path checks only. A successful local check means *well-formed candidate*, not *true claim*.

### F-03 — Graph-as-index conflicts with the canonical substrate decision

The comparative review calls the graph reconstructible condensate. The later Constitution makes
the Concept Graph the principal durable graph/index substrate. CEO-1 follows the Constitution
while keeping executable-library research available as an alternative future substrate proposal.

### F-04 — Rich concepts hide four separate lifecycles

The hybrid object combines semantic identity, representation revisions, operator versions, and
runtime execution. CEO-1 separates them so each can be versioned and audited without mutable
in-place objects.

### F-05 — Novelty and confidence are repeatedly conflated

Novelty, promise, relevance, coherence, and truth confidence are different quantities. CEO-1
stores score vectors and explanations, never a single authoritative `confidence` field. Missing
signals remain missing and weights are renormalized explicitly.

### F-06 — Failure retention is a constitutional asset

The archive's rejected candidates and alternatives are valuable negative knowledge. CEO-1 uses
append-only candidate revisions and disposition references; no failed analogy or hypothesis is
deleted merely because it did not validate.

## 7. Open research question dispositions

### 7.1 Resolved for CEO-1

| Question | CEO-1 answer |
|---|---|
| What is a concept? | An immutable semantic node with typed representation facets and provenance. |
| Is a graph the correct abstraction? | It is the required durable topology substrate, but not the entirety of reasoning or truth. |
| Should concepts execute code? | No. Versioned `Transformation` operators carry behavior; the store executes nothing. |
| Should concepts own memory or run continuously? | No. History belongs to append-only records and exploration traces; execution is demand-driven. |
| Can concepts merge, split, or rewrite themselves? | Only by proposing new immutable nodes and typed derivation edges; never by in-place mutation. |
| How are n-ary relations represented? | By reified nodes (`Analogy`, `Transformation`, `Hypothesis`, `SemanticNeighborhood`) plus typed edges. |
| Should search algorithms cooperate? | Yes, through one protocol, deterministic budgets, and a shared deduplicated candidate pool. |
| Should algorithms compete for resources? | A deterministic configured allocator in CEO-1; learned allocation is deferred. |
| How is novelty measured? | As an explainable, versioned score vector over distance, structural surprise, domain span, rarity, and duplication; never as truth. |
| How is usefulness measured? | Query relevance, constraint coherence, diversity contribution, and later externally observed outcomes remain separate fields. |
| Should every discovery require symbolic proof? | No. Concept OS only produces candidates. PAEOS and SAKG select evidence and warrant appropriate to the domain. |
| How do failed discoveries affect search? | They remain addressable and may inform versioned heuristics without being deleted or treated as proof. |
| How should uncertainty propagate? | Preserve component scores, missing-signal masks, assumptions, and provenance; do not collapse them into truth confidence. |
| What executes in parallel? | Search arms may run concurrently, but deterministic seeds, budgets, merge order, and tie-breaking make outputs replayable. |

### 7.2 Deferred hypotheses

- native hypergraph storage rather than reification;
- autonomous or continuously active concepts;
- arbitrary executable hypothesis libraries;
- MDL as the global objective and compression-progress curiosity;
- wake/sleep consolidation, pruning, or forgetting;
- learned search allocation and automatically invented search algorithms;
- automatic concept merging/splitting beyond candidate proposals;
- grounding concepts from raw perception;
- causal/interventional discovery and experiment design;
- federated or social multi-instance knowledge;
- sharding and global distribution; and
- non-stationary expiry policies.

Each deferred item requires an explicit hypothesis, benchmark, falsifier, and PAEOS-governed
experiment before it changes CEO-1.

### 7.3 Rejected or forbidden in CEO-1

- a single search algorithm as the whole reasoning system;
- direct model/provider calls from Concept OS;
- neural scores as verification;
- transitive analogy inference by default;
- mutable graph rewrites, deletes, or silent edge strengthening;
- frequency-based promotion;
- Concept OS statuses named `validated`, `proven`, or `canonical truth`;
- direct writes to SAKG's claim store; and
- any path from a Concept OS candidate to Sentium execution without Sentium risk controls and
  PAEOS admission.

## 8. CEO-1 readiness and acceptance boundary

Mission 0 supports implementation of a deliberately small first architecture:

- pure SQLite append-only storage in `concept/store.py` with WAL enabled;
- deterministic content-derived Crockford Base32 `concept://` URIs;
- the six required node types and six required edge families;
- typed representation facets and explicit transformation operators;
- deterministic traversal and semantic-neighborhood snapshots;
- an Inspiration Engine for analogy, recombination, novelty, and serendipity;
- a budgeted Search Orchestra with replayable traces;
- a structurally read-only MCP query surface; and
- a candidate-only, PAEOS-mediated SAKG emission bridge.

This census does not claim that Concept OS generates useful discoveries, that its scores are
calibrated, or that any hypothesis has been validated. Those are implementation and empirical
questions assigned to the execution DAG and later Court evidence.

---

## 9. Mission 0 falsifiers

CEO-1 should be remanded or revised if implementation cannot demonstrate all of the following:

1. identical canonical semantic input yields the same `concept://` URI across processes;
2. stored domain records cannot be updated or deleted through the storage API or raw SQL;
3. a query is replayable from its query hash, graph snapshot hash, algorithm versions, and seeds;
4. missing embeddings or scores are explicit rather than silently coerced;
5. analogies are non-transitive unless a new analogy is explicitly proposed;
6. every hypothesis has a complete derivation/recombination/analogy trace and declared falsifier;
7. the MCP surface has no reachable mutation method;
8. a poisoned high-novelty candidate cannot acquire a validated SAKG state; and
9. no Concept OS path can invoke a provider or execute a Sentium action outside PAEOS.

These are non-vacuous boundaries: failure is evidence against the architecture, not a reason to
weaken the acceptance criterion.
